Flagship Asset: Sombrero

⛰️

Sombrero Project — Andahuaylas-Yauri Belt, Peru

~56,400 Hectares — Adjacent to Las Bambas, Haquira & Tintaya
Location Context

Elephant Country

The Andahuaylas-Yauri belt hosts some of the world's largest copper mines. Coppernico's land sits directly adjacent to Las Bambas, Haquira, and Tintaya — proven Tier 1 deposits that validate the geological potential of the entire belt.

The "Cover" Theory

Hidden Beneath the Sombrero

The accessible copper in this belt has largely been found and mined. Coppernico's geologists believe the same massive copper-gold skarn systems extend onto their land but are concealed under a "sombrero" (hat) of younger volcanic rock — never properly drilled.

Scale & Potential

District-Scale Target

At 56,400 hectares, this is not a single mine target. A successful discovery would constitute a district-scale, Tier 1 asset. The geological setting demands a large-scale approach, which is exactly what the current UAV survey program is designed to provide.

Recent Catalyst — Dec 4, 2025

UAV Magnetic & Gravity Survey

Launched a 13,000-hectare UAV magnetic and gravity survey — effectively an X-ray through the volcanic cover. The goal: pinpoint the dense copper-gold bodies beneath for precision drill targeting. Survey results are the next major market catalyst.

The Copper Squeeze

Why the macro backdrop amplifies the Coppernico opportunity right now

The Citi / JP Morgan Scenario

Copper is the metal of electrification — EVs, grid infrastructure, data centers, and renewables all require it at scale. Analysts project a structural supply deficit building through 2026–2030 as demand from the energy transition outpaces mining capacity additions.

If copper hits $5.90/lb, major miners (BHP, Rio Tinto, Glencore) will be under immense shareholder pressure to replace depleting reserves. Greenfield discoveries in proven belts — exactly what Coppernico is hunting — would command acquisition premiums far above normal.

This is the core of the thesis: a small-cap explorer with a plausible Tier 1 target in a proven belt, backed by two major miners who know exactly what sits next door, positioned ahead of a potential copper super-cycle.

06

The Three Killers

Risks that could take this stock near zero — size your position accordingly

Risk 01 — Geological

Exploration Failure

The "cover" theory might simply be wrong. The volcanic rock could be masking nothing but barren geology. A dry drill program — holes that miss significant mineralization — is the terminal scenario for any junior explorer. Result: stock goes near zero.

Risk 02 — Jurisdictional

Permitting Hell

Peru is a notoriously difficult permitting jurisdiction. Bureaucratic delays can stall projects for years. If the expanded drill permit slides from mid-2026 to late 2026 or beyond, the company burns cash without results, straining the balance sheet and investor patience.

Risk 03 — Social

Community Relations

Local communities in this belt have historically blocked roads to Las Bambas, halting operations at one of the world's largest copper mines. Coppernico currently has signed access agreements and good community relations — but this is always fragile and can deteriorate rapidly.

The 2025 Strategic Mandate

Coppernico is a pure-play bet on one of the best-located untested copper-gold targets in South America. The combination of proven belt geology, a credible "cover" theory, a management team with two prior exits, and strategic validation from Teck and Newmont creates a compelling — if highly speculative — setup.

The stock belongs in the venture capital sleeve of a copper-weighted portfolio, sized as a high-risk satellite position alongside stable producers like Ivanhoe or Teck. The drilling catalyst in late 2026 is the binary event. If copper is at $5.90/lb when those drills turn, the market will be watching very closely.

The window to build a position ahead of the permit approval and geophysics results is right now — at a ~$50M market cap, the downside is partially de-risked by the cash position, and the upside is district-scale.
