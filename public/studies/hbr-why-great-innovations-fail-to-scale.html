The Problem

As gen AI performs more creative and interpersonal tasks, many workers feel their competence, autonomy, and sense of connection at work are under threat — leading to resistance, disengagement, and even sabotage.

The Solution

Use the AWARE framework to integrate gen AI at work: Acknowledge worker concerns, Watch for maladaptive coping, Align support systems, Redesign roles, and Empower workers through transparency.

The Payoff

By supporting workers' core psychological needs, organizations can increase receptivity to gen AI — transforming it from a perceived threat into a trusted copilot.

Psychological Impact

🎯
Competence

The feeling of being effective and capable. Gen AI can boost skills across experience levels — but also threatens workers when it redefines needed capabilities or signals replacement. 89% of senior leaders say it enhances skills; 71% also worry it will lead to skill atrophy.

↑ Boosts capability ↓ Threatens identity
🎛️
Autonomy

The feeling of being in control. AI can reduce administrative burden and free workers for meaningful tasks — but mandating it creates an "algorithmic cage" that limits workers' ability to tailor their work, threatening professional identity and ownership.

↑ Reduces cognitive load ↓ Erodes ownership
🤝
Relatedness

The feeling of meaningful interpersonal connection. Gen AI can free time for social work and even provide human-like warmth — but automating collaborative tasks can evoke loneliness, reduce team cohesion, and spark generational resentment.

↑ Frees time for people ↓ Disrupts social fabric

"If we can have some protections where we, the workers, can control the automation, then the automation can be used to help us do our jobs. We're not saying we want to go back to the rotary phone. We're saying we need to hold the keys."

— Raphael Bob-Waksberg, creator of BoJack Horseman, on the 2023 writers' strike

The AWARE Framework

To help leaders facilitate the adoption of gen AI in the workplace, the authors designed five interdependent actions. As gen AI capabilities evolve, so must the application of these principles — this is not a one-time checklist but a continuous leadership posture.

A
Acknowledge
How gen AI may affect workers' feelings of competence, autonomy, and relatedness.
Create space for open dialogue. Surface concerns — don't suppress them. Build psychological safety before rolling out tools.
W
Watch
For maladaptive behaviors so you can intervene before performance declines.
Monitor task avoidance, withdrawal, shadow AI use. 41% of Gen Z workers admit actively working against AI initiatives.
A
Align
Support systems with the psychological needs of workers.
Offer personalized learning journeys, peer coaching, role-specific paths. One-size-fits-all programs fail. Only 36% feel properly trained today.
R
Redesign
Workflows to make the most of human–AI collaboration.
Focus on end-to-end workflow redesign, not plug-and-play. Give AI repetitive tasks; give humans empathy, creativity, ethical judgment.
E
Empower
Workers to shape how gen AI affects their work lives.
Be transparent about what AI will do and how roles may evolve. Only 44% of leaders currently involve workers in implementation.

Who's Doing It Right

GitHub
By monitoring adoption and usage metrics of GitHub Copilot, managers assess onboarding efforts and track how developers engage with the tool — detecting both adaptive engagement and early signs of disengagement for timely support. A model for the Watch step.
Citigroup
Created the Citi AI suite expanded to 80+ markets for 175,000 employees. More than 2,000 employees participate in AI Champions and Accelerator programs to ensure peer support — a best-practice example of the Align step.
Moderna
Merged its Technology and HR departments into People and Digital Technology — a single unit designed to co-create AI workflows and collaboratively decide what stays human-led vs automated across trials, staffing, and operations. Pure Redesign.
Dell
Before introducing gen AI tools, simplified sales processes, consolidated content, and eliminated redundancies. Bringing in AI at that point amplified efficiency further and freed sales teams for strategic customer engagement — workflow-first thinking.
BNY
"We want AI to be able to be for everyone in the company" — CEO Robin Vince. ~60% of employees onboarded themselves to the gen AI platform; 5,000 — including half the engineering team — have already built their own agents. The Empower step at scale.
PwC
The My AI initiative combines tools, training, and a dedicated "playground" with "prompting parties" where employees experiment in real contexts. Peer "activators" embedded across the firm ensure support is woven into day-to-day work, not siloed in L&D.
Duolingo
After its "AI-first" strategy caused anxiety, CEO Luis von Ahn publicly acknowledged the misstep: "AI is creating uncertainty for all of us, and we can respond with fear or curiosity." He reframed the change and affirmed AI would not replace employees — a textbook Acknowledge move.
Gen AI is not merely a tool.
Workers are not merely users.

The gen AI revolution is redefining the boundary between people and machines. Its human-like capabilities make workers view it not just as a new technological system but as a social actor — even a teammate — in the workplace. As a result, leaders can't think about integrating gen AI in purely technical, operational, or cost-efficiency terms.

The AWARE framework enables leaders to respect the psychological realities of change, foster psychological safety, and ensure that the technology augments, rather than replaces, human workers. They should be partners in co-creating the future of work.
